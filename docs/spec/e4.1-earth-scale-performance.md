# E4.1 — Earth-scale performance: the globe, the character creator, loading into the world

*The owner's fix pass of 2026-10-08, as given, inserted ahead of everything else
(`PLAN.md`). It is a "Fast" failure under the Quality Charter (Amendment Q §1, §7).*

The owner playtested the latest build and found three serious problems that most likely come
from the move to an Earth-sized world (Amendment E §5). The goal is not only to make these
screens fast again: it is to make the Earth-sized world run well while staying as realistic as
possible, and to make sure it cannot regress.

## 1. What the owner reports
1. The globe is always all blue, and selecting a place on it manually is far too laggy.
2. The character creator is slow: the character takes too long to update when settings change.
3. Loading into the world takes far too long: it maxes out the CPU until the game cannot load in,
   and it crashes.

Each goes into `dev/PLAYTEST.md` with its reproduction, its measured cause and its fix.

## 2. What the code suggests (confirm against the current tree before fixing)
- The globe's map calls the full block-level sampler for every texel: `globe.rs`
  `planet_map()` calls `terrain.sample()` for 2048 × 1024 points over the whole planet; at Earth
  size each call lands somewhere new and, with the refinement levels (E §5.2), may build
  fine-scale tiles: enough to keep the map from ever finishing (so the globe stays blue) and to
  eat the CPU and memory.
- Hovering does it again on every mouse move: `describe()` samples the point under the cursor,
  `start_at()` searches for a spawn with `spawn_near()`; detail lookups for the suggested places
  (E §6.3) on hover would be worse.
- Parallel work competes for every core: rayon's global pool across the worldgen, server, scene
  and globe, plus a pool in `lod_stream.rs`; nothing reserves a core for the main thread
  (input, UI, rendering).
- Precision at Earth size: f32 holds whole metres exactly only to about ±16.7 million; Earth's X
  runs to about 40 million. World-space f32 math (noise inputs, positions, distances, LOD tile
  origins) and i32 products (squared distances: 40M² overflows) misbehave.
- Possible memory blow-up: fine refinement tiles or LOD and far-field requests over a large
  radius without strict LRU caps; a planet grid built at a much higher resolution.

## 3. Diagnose first
Measure every problem, do not guess:
- Instrument: `tracy` zones on world creation, loading, the globe, the creator and terrain
  generation; counters for fine-tile builds per second by caller; cache sizes per refinement
  level; peak resident memory; per-thread CPU time; queue lengths in every job queue.
- Reproduce headlessly with bench commands on the Earth-sized planet: `hearth bench globe` (map
  build time; hover latency over 1,000 random points; click-to-details time); `hearth bench
  creator` (time from a setting change to the updated preview over scripted slider drags, on
  the software adapter if there is no GPU); `hearth bench load` (time from "Play" to the player
  in control, peak memory, CPU per thread, the stages and how long each takes; a new world and an
  existing save).
- Crashes: a panic hook logging the message and backtrace to the log file; capture
  out-of-memory, GPU device-lost or oversized-buffer errors and stack overflows; record which it
  was; make the bench reproduce it.
- The findings, with numbers, in `dev/PLAYTEST.md` before changing code.

## 4. The fixes and the rules behind them
### 4.1 Every query at the scale it needs
- One query API with a footprint, e.g. `sample(x, z, footprint_m)`, reading the coarsest
  refinement level at least as fine as the footprint: the planet grid (~20 km) for the globe,
  climate summaries and coarse searches; the ~2.5 km or ~300 m levels for regional searches,
  the far field and distant LOD and ecology cells; the ~40 m level for mid LOD; full 1 m detail
  for cube generation and the near field only.
- Audit every caller of the full-detail `Terrain::sample()` and move each to its scale: the
  globe, place descriptions, spawn and start-place search and verification, LOD and far-field
  tiles, ecology habitat setup, rivers and seasonal cover, weather, the suggested places.
- Guard it: a debug-build counter (and a test) fails if anything but cube generation and
  near-field systems builds fine tiles; the perf gate reports fine-tile builds per caller.

### 4.2 The globe
- Its map straight from the planet grid (elevation, water, climate and biome class, relief
  shading), well under a second, built with the planet and saved with the planet cache: never
  blank once the planet exists.
- Land shows as land: a test that the map's land fraction matches the grid's (about 29 % on the
  Earth-like planet) and that the map is not uniform.
- Hover is instant: coarse fields bilinear from the grid (latitude and longitude, elevation,
  climate class, biome, temperature, precipitation) within the frame; no fine sampling, no
  searching.
- A click starts the details asynchronously: the start-spot search and the "what to look for"
  checks (E §6.3) in the background at the coarsest scale that answers each; a small "Looking
  closer…" indicator; results cached per region; cancelled when the player clicks elsewhere.
  Full detail is generated only where the player finally spawns.
- The suggested places follow the same rule (E's ≤ ~15 s for all of them is a ceiling).
- Targets: rotation and zoom ≥ 60 FPS; hover within one frame (< 1 ms CPU); the map the moment
  the planet exists; click-to-details typically ≤ 1 s.

### 4.3 The character creator
- Changes show at once: dragging a slider updates the preview every frame at ≥ 60 FPS (≤ ~3 ms
  CPU an update): slider events coalesced (latest wins); only what changed updated (morph
  weights, colours and parameters as uniforms, no full rebuild); the figure and its GPU buffers
  kept between changes.
- Anything expensive (skin or hair textures, hair cards for a new style, E §8) asynchronous: an
  immediate approximate update, the refined result swapped in when ready, cached by parameters.
- Nothing in the background starves it (§4.6): world or planet preparation runs at low priority.
- The preview renders at a sensible resolution with its own lightweight pipeline, not the world
  renderer behind the menus.

### 4.4 Loading into the world
- Progressive: control as soon as the immediate surroundings are ready (the ground under and
  around the player for collision, light and rendering, a short radius of cubes, the nearest LOD
  ring); the rest streams afterwards, nearest and in view first, with fade-ins.
- Each stage bounded: each ring at the scale it needs (§4.1); far field and distant LOD from
  coarse levels.
- The loading screen shows stages with honest progress, stays responsive (≥ 30 FPS, the window
  movable, a Cancel button back to the menu), never blocks the main thread.
- Saves load faster than new worlds: generated refinement tiles near the player's area cached on
  disk (deterministic, versioned, size-capped); the planet and globe caches kept.
- Targets on the reference machine (8 cores, RTX 3060 / RX 6600 class), "Play" to control:
  ≤ ~10 s for a new world (planet existing), ≤ ~5 s for a save; the full render distance within
  ~30 s while playable. The owner's machine class recorded too (§6).

### 4.5 Precision and overflow at Earth size
- Audit world-space math for f32 precision (positions, noise, distances, LOD and far-field tile
  origins, shader inputs, physics): f64 for world positions, tile- or camera-relative f32
  elsewhere (the floating origin extended to every system).
- Noise and hashing on tile-local coordinates with integer tile ids, wrapping at the seam.
- Integer overflow at 40-million-block coordinates (squared distances, areas, index math): i64
  where needed; tests near the planet's extremes (X near the seam, Z near the poles).

### 4.6 Threads and jobs: never starve the player
- One job system instead of rayon's global pool used ad hoc: priorities (interactive >
  near-field > LOD > far-field > background preparation); bounded queues, cancelling work for
  places the camera has left; capacity reserved for the main thread, rendering and audio (one
  core, two on 8+), the worker count set accordingly and configurable.
- Long background jobs split into small chunks so higher priorities preempt them.
- CPU per thread measured in the benches; the main thread keeps its frame budget while loading.

### 4.7 Memory: hard caps
- Strict budgets with LRU eviction for every cache (refinement tiles by level, cubes, meshes,
  LOD tiles, far field, ecology regions, textures), sized from the machine: together at most
  about 40 % of physical RAM by default, degrading by evicting harder and lowering distant detail
  rather than growing.
- Memory by category in F3 and in `BENCHMARKS.md`.
- Nothing per-planet at fine resolution: anything sized by circumference or area is the coarse
  grid or lazily per tile.
- A soak test flies across the Earth-sized planet for 30 minutes; memory stays flat within the
  cap.

### 4.8 Realistic terrain that runs well
- Hierarchical refinement done efficiently: each level's tile from its parent plus local
  processes (uplift and rock, drainage-guided erosion, detail); fast erosion approximations at
  the fine levels, full erosion iteration at the coarse levels; SIMD inner loops; GPU compute
  where available (the CPU path the reference and the server's).
- Fine detail only where seen up close; mid and far distances from coarser levels with the same
  macro structure.
- Disk caching of computed tiles near places the player has been.
- Realism re-checked after optimizing: the Earth-scale worldgen statistics (E §11), the terrain
  screenshot set (ranges, coastlines, valleys, plains, from the ground and a summit), before and
  after in `docs/review/earth-scale-performance.md`. A shortcut that changes how the land looks
  must look the same at the distance it is used.
