# Decisions D300–D399

Full texts; the index is `DECISIONS.md`.

## D300 — The natural trees drawn as their meshes; a tree cut into drawn as its blocks; a felled tree falls as its mesh
S5/P7 (c) (Amendment S §7.1, RGA-1's tree gap). **The natural trees about the player are drawn
as their meshes** (`hearth::tree_view`), not as their blocks. The server leaves each one's
blocks out of the cubes' meshes where a block still holds the state the generator grew there
(`LocalWorld::mesh`, `scene::blank_trees`); the voxels stay in the world for light, collision,
felling and the ecology. The client, the screenshot tool and the bench find the same trees from
the generator and the vegetation alone (`Features::drawn_trees_about`, and `trees_in` a column at
a time on worker threads), so server and client agree without a message per tree. A tree's mesh
is keyed by its species, stage and variant, by what is left of it (living, snag, charred,
stump) and by detail. The detail goes by distance: full within 24 m, reduced to 80 m, low beyond
(`hearth_flora::mesh`). Meshes are made off the main thread and kept on the GPU, a coarser one
drawn while a finer is made.

Each tree is drawn as an instance:
- at its skeleton's origin, with the turn its blocks were turned by (`PlacedTree::frame`, the
  affine map of `Turn::apply_f`): the stem drawn stands within 0.75 m of its blocks;
- with its column's climate (its leaves' tint and fall) and the seasonal cover's snow on it;
- lit by the sky beside its foot for its stem (its own blocks are dark within) and above its
  crown for its limbs and leaves, and by the block light;
- with its sway's own phase.

**A tree cut into is drawn as its blocks** (`LocalWorld::cut_into`). A change by the player or a
fire that leaves one of its blocks other than as the generator grew it marks the tree cut in the
vegetation: by its foot, as what it then is (species, stage and what is left, `PlacedTree::what`).
The mark is saved, and sent with the vegetation at once, before the tree's cubes come again
meshed with all its blocks. It holds until the tree there is another (grown on, burned, felled):
that tree is drawn whole again.

**A felled tree falls as its mesh** (`TreeFalls` carries its foot; `TreeView::fell`):
- What falls is all of it but its stump: the mesh cut 1 m up, the cut capped
  (`hearth_flora::mesh::Options::above`).
- It turns down about the felling cut's edge through the fall's quarter turn, faster as it goes
  (`TreeInstance::tilt`, an angle and a level axis, applied in `tree.wgsl`).
- The vegetation's felling is told first, so the stump is drawn as its own mesh once the column is
  found again.
- A tree already drawn as its blocks falls as its blocks.

**The tree looked at is outlined by its own mesh** (T §2.3, D295): the tree pass draws it into
the highlight's mask, as drawn and swaying, its wood or its leaves cut to their shapes
(`tree_mask.wgsl`), instead of the block's quads (a cube's outline about a round stem).

Trees beyond the render distance stay the distant terrain's crowns (L1).

Measured on the same build (`hearth bench`, `--trees blocks` for before; `BENCHMARKS.md`):
- The forest flight: draws 5,316 → 2,461; triangles 1.54 → 0.88 M; video memory 360 →
  289 MiB.
- The new rainforest walk (`rainforest_walk`, §7.4): draws 6,634 → 3,194; triangles 2.55 →
  1.53 M; video memory 418 → 352 MiB.
- The shadow cascades' draws are counted in both. Each near cascade draws only the trees that
  may cast into it.
- The trees' own work on the frame thread: 0.73–0.79 ms on the cloud machine with the camera
  moving at 80 m/s, most of it new columns and meshes arriving. The trees are culled by column
  before tree by tree, and the columns are looked over again only when something changed.
- The near terrain's quads at head height in temperate rainforest: 246k → 15k, the trees' own
  drawn as 185 instances.

Rejected:
- Each tree's mesh baked into the cubes' meshes: a tree spans a dozen cubes, and each would carry
  its share at every detail.
- The client asking the server which trees to draw: a message per tree and column, and the two
  could disagree.
- A tree cut into drawn as its mesh less what was taken: the mesh is the skeleton's, not the
  blocks'.

Not yet:
- The look meets a tree by its blocks, near its drawn shape; it should meet the mesh, as should
  collision (by the skeleton's capsules, S §7.1).
- The falling crown breaking its twigs, and the fallen trunk lying as a log's mesh (S5/P7 (e)).
- Trees beyond the near field as instances, then impostors (L1).

## D301 — The walking scale: a level of ~5 m cells for the blocks alone, dissected and drained; the ground's own roughness below it; every other read a stand-in
G1b (T §4, RGA-1's gaps 3, 5 and 9). Below 38 m the land was G1a's noise: hills of bumps, not
ridges and valleys; up to an eighth of the ground in closed hollows; the desert's and boreal
land's curvature at 4 m a third to a half of lidar's.

**A walking level** (`relief/walking.rs`): a fourth refinement level of 4.8 m cells, made by the
same tiles as the others (drainage, erosion, creep, slump) and read only by the blocks about the
players (`Terrain::nearby`; `level_for` stops above it). Its own parts:
- Its erosion reckons with the storms' runoff on dry land's bare ground (0.3 of the rain at
  150 mm and less: Yair and Lavee 1985), where real dry lands drain densest; the discharge it keeps
  for rivers is the mean's.
- G1a's burial of dry basins' relief stops above it: its relief is the fill's own surface, washes,
  bars and swales (Bull 1977). Burying its longest octave on basin floors was tried: one desert
  window fell to 0.63 of lidar's curvature, the other barely changed.
- A second drainage pass over tiles offset half a tile from the first's cuts the dams the first
  pass's blend leaves between tiles.
- It drains along its rows and columns, and its channels (brooks from 0.0005 m³/s) step only
  along them: the blocks read between its cells, and a cell draining only across a corner,
  between two higher, holds a hollow there, a channel stepping so dams itself.
- Its tiles are made side by side about a world's spawn before its first cubes want them (160
  blocks).

**Each level's finer octaves laid across the slope beneath them** (`relief/gullies.rs`): a
lattice of gullies running downhill, scaled to the isotropic noise's spread, where the slope
falls further over an octave's wavelength than its relief reaches; isotropic on gentle ground.
Below 76 m soil-mantled ground's relief falls away a power faster (Perron et al. 2008); bare rock,
young glacial ground and dry land keep theirs.

**Reads between cells stay between them** (`monotone`, Fritsch and Carlson 1980): Catmull-Rom
with its slopes held in, the same where the values rise or fall steadily; plain Catmull-Rom dips
below a valley's floor two cells wide by an eighth of its sides' rise.

**The ground's own roughness below the cells** (`Terrain::micro_relief`): soil's mounds and pits
(0.65 m: roots, tree-throw, burrows), smooth on the flattest ground; on dry land's slopes stony
knobs that ring no hollow (to 4 m by outcrop and slope); bare rock above the trees and on cliffs
(6 m); none on a channel's bed, which its water keeps bare (on it they dammed the brooks and the
ponds draining to them). It replaces G1a's blocks' noise (wavelengths 96–12 m at full strength
in every read).

**Every other read takes a stand-in** (`Relief::walking_proxy`): the walking level's octaves over
its parent's surface, without its drainage, those finer than the columns' spacing faded out as a
DEM's samples average them. The distant view reads it on the 38 m level's cells; at every
measured window its slope at 30 m lies within half a degree of the blocks' own averaged to 30 m.
Placement (deposits, springs, caves, a place's card) reads the 38 m level at the blocks' scale
with the stand-in and the micro relief (`Terrain::sample_regional`), within some metres of the
blocks' own (`REGIONAL_SLACK_M`, 24 m; a fifth of a metre at the median): a spring's pool is
found on the blocks' surface when a cube it may reach is made, a body's signs and a sinkhole's
shaft are looked for within the slack. Reads of many cells hold their
tiles for the run (`Run`) rather than asking the shared cache for each.

**Drenched land's hillslopes set again** (`roughness.rs`, 0.75 → 0.95): G1a's 30 m reads had
counted the blocks' noise, aliased between columns 30 m apart, as slope; read faithfully, hill
country stood at 16.1° against the Coast Range's 21.8°. Now its windows read 21.2–21.6° at 30 m
(lidar 21.6–22.2°), the site 17.3°. The tile store's format is 4: tiles kept by earlier builds are
made again; a world saved before keeps its planet but not its relief (as D299).

Measured (seed 7; `bench realism terrain` with G1a at the same windows, `REALISM_WINDOWS`;
`docs/review/g1b/`):
- |Δh| at 1–64 m and the 4 m curvature within × 1.5 of the kind's lidar in the hills (0.97–1.13),
  the desert (0.68–1.34), boreal land (0.82–0.99) and the plains' first window (0.90–1.24); the
  plains' second, 0.48–0.56, lies between the kind's two lidar windows (|Δh| at 4 m 0.26 m against
  0.19 and 0.51); mountains 0.66–1.32, their 64 m relief just short. G1a at the same windows:
  0.33–1.27 outside the mountains (1.6–2.9 in them).
- Closed hollows over 0.25 m: 0.0–0.7 % of the ground off rivers outside the mountains (G1a
  0.8–12 %); glacial mountains 3.3–5.5 %; a carved river's trough 1.3 % of one hills window.
- The planet's windows within their bounds: median slope 2.5° (2.8°), relief 170 m (163), land
  over 5° 31 % (33 %); slope at 30 m by kind within ± 25 % but the desert's, 5.5° against 4.2° (the
  stand-in's octaves on its fans; its form is G1a's open item).
- Load, both disk caches cleared (`BENCHMARKS.md`): places 7.2–7.3 s (7.7–8.4), a new world in
  control 4.8–4.9 s (5.0–5.3), its save 2.8–3.1 s (2.8); the whole render distance 33–36 s here
  (28–29): the distant tiles finer where the land is rougher, drawn by llvmpipe. A new world makes
  16 walking tiles (2.1 MiB), 52 ms each, the second pass 1.5 ms.

Rejected:
- A level of metre cells: 23 times the walking level's, its tiles far beyond the load budget; the
  octaves and the micro relief give the metre's roughness at the blocks.
- Placement reading the walking level: 48 more of its tiles for a new world, and some 40 s for
  the menus' five places.
- Filling the blocks' hollows after the fact: a cube cannot see where a hollow's outlet lies.

Not yet:
- The mountains' kilometre-scale relief (|Δh| at 64 m 0.66 of lidar's) and their grain at a
  metre; the desert's fans rough and its ranges low; the plains' steep valley sides (Iowa's).
- Rivers whose banks stand below their water (W1); lakes with stepped straight shores (W1).
- Frost, wind and solution forms, fans and talus as forms of their own, clasts by lithology and
  soil surfaces (S5/P7 (e), P7G).
