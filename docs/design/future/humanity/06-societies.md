# 6. Societies: cooperation, institutions, economy and conflict

*Amendment E §10.6.*

## Households
Shared stores and work; division of labour by role, skill and culture; the household's plans
(the harvest, the move to summer pasture) made by its heads' minds or, at C1, by routine.

## Cooperation
- **Joint intentions and commitments:** who does what by when, recorded; group tasks (hunts,
  harvests, building) allocate roles by negotiation (C3–C4) or bidding (C2: each member's cost
  for each role, the cheapest assignment).
- **A ledger of reciprocity and obligation** per relationship and household.
- **Reputation** spread by gossip along relationships, attenuated and sometimes wrong.
- **Replanning together** when a step fails: the commitment is reopened, not silently dropped.

## Institutions as collective agents
Households, councils, chiefs, temples, guilds, markets, courts and armies, each with **rules**
(data: membership, offices, how decisions are made, what they may decide) and a **decision
procedure**. Near players, or when it matters, a deliberation is a structured multi-party
model session, each member arguing from their own interests and character; elsewhere the
numeric models resolve it.

## Economy
Household production and consumption; markets with prices from supply and demand; barter to
money by era; traders travelling real routes with real loads; scarcity raising the risk of
conflict. Flows between places follow gravity-model estimates on the route graph until people
are instantiated, then the traders themselves.

## Settlements grow because people build
Sites chosen and building forms taken from the culture's vocabulary; materials gathered and
pieces built with the real construction and structural systems (V2-8, S6); roads emerge where
people walk (the ground's wear, then made roads by institutions).

## Conflict
- **Interpersonal:** an escalation ladder (words, threats, shoves) ending in real punches,
  kicks and tackles through the Actor.
- **Feuds and raids** tracked as obligations and grievances between households and polities.
- **Wars:** armies are collective agents moving on the route graph; battles near players are
  fought by individuals, elsewhere resolved statistically by terrain, technology, numbers and
  supply.

## Pathfinding at every scale
| Scale | Method |
|---|---|
| Long journeys | the region graph (least-cost routes, roads preferred) |
| Within a region | hierarchical navigation over S3's smooth-terrain grid |
| Crowds and armies | flow fields (continuum crowds, Treuille et al. 2006) |
| Close quarters | reciprocal velocity obstacles (ORCA, van den Berg et al. 2011) |
| Special links | climb, swim, ford, boat, ladder, door |
Paths are cached and shared within groups; every scale has a per-tick budget.
