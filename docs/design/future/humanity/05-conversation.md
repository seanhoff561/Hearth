# 5. Talking: real-time conversation and voice

*Amendment E §10.5.*

## Turn-taking (System 1)
When to speak, listen, interrupt, keep silent or murmur agreement; conversations in groups of
players and people; recognising who is addressed (gaze, name, position, the last speaker).
Silence is always an option, and often the right one.

## What to say (C4)
Streamed from the conversation model, conditioned on the person's character, memories,
beliefs, relationships, mood, culture, the era's vocabulary and their language. Every line is
filtered for **knowledge** (they say only what they could know: the World Bible through their
reach) and for **safety** (`10-safety.md`) before it is spoken. A line is a proposal like any
other: it is recorded in the decision journal and changes beliefs, never facts.

## Voice
- Streaming speech recognition for players; streaming synthesis with a **stable voice per
  person** derived from age, sex, body and an inherited timbre, with emotional prosody, the
  accent of their culture, whispering and shouting (sound that carries, as in R5).
- Lip sync and facial animation from the phonemes and the appraisal.
- **Latency:** first audio in about 0.5–0.8 s; the wait covered by natural filler (a look, a
  breath, "hm") chosen by System 1.

## Languages
The generated languages return (the archive's phonologies, sound change and word replacement).
- *Authentic:* people speak their own language, lines rendered through its lexicon and grammar
  and spoken by phoneme synthesis, with subtitles translated as far as the player understands.
- *Understood:* people speak the player's language with an accent (accessibility).
- The player learns a language by living among its speakers: understanding grows word by word
  (the knowledge engine's practice, applied to vocabulary).

## What it builds on
Amendment R's AI Bridge (provider adapters, the Agent Bridge protocol, budgets, privacy) and
its agent voices (R4, R6) move into Phase F as F6 and F7 (E §9.3).
