# The player is born

*V2.1 Addendum A (user direction, 2026-10-03); D163–D164. Milestones: H1 (the genome from two
parents, no appearance chosen), H3 (born into a family, the childhood), H8 (birth options from
the area's peoples), H9 (stepping in from the Observer as a birth).*

The player does not arrive grown and made to measure. They are born — a baby of two parents who
live in the world — grow up in that family and its people, and come of age as an adult, quicker
than life but through the same stages. What they look like comes from their parents' genes; what
they know as an adult is what they were taught and found out as a child.

## Choosing a birth

After the globe (v2 §16: the planet, the era, a place on it), the game offers **two to four
births** at that place, drawn from the people who actually live within reach of it in that era:

- Each is a **household** of the area — real people of the household tier with their histories
  (V2.1 §15.2): the mother and the father (names, ages, what each does), brothers and sisters,
  grandparents if living, the band, village or town they belong to, their people and way of life
  (§9), the place they live.
- The options differ in **way of life** as far as the area allows: families of two peoples where
  peoples meet; a fisher's family on the shore and a hunter's in the hills; a herding family and
  a farming one in a Neolithic valley; a chief's household and a commoner's in a chiefdom. A
  remote place may offer two families of one band.
- The player picks one, and chooses whether to be born a **daughter or a son** or leaves it to
  chance (the father's gamete is drawn conditioned on it; nothing else is chosen). The parents
  name the child in their language (§10.1); the player may keep the name or give another.
- **Appearance is never chosen and never previewed.** The child's genome is the meiosis of the
  two parents' genomes (§4.2), drawn from the world's seed and the household when the birth is
  confirmed; the phenotype — skin, hair, eyes, face, height to come — unfolds from it and from
  the childhood (§4.4). The parents are shown; the child will look like a believable mix of them.

## Wild Earth's families

Wild Earth (the default era) has no peoples, only sporadic *Australopithecus* (v2 §8). So that
every player is born, Wild Earth holds **a few wandering families of people** — the era's one
liberty besides the hominins, as the player always was: households of four to ten (parents,
children, perhaps a grandparent), far apart, living by foraging. They know what living where they
live takes and no more (their region's knowledge baseline in the era profile): where it is warm,
the stone hammer, sharp flakes and cutting with them, the digging stick, throwing stones,
carrying bundles and tracking; where it is cold, also keeping and making fire, hide wraps and a
windbreak. Everything beyond is the player's to discover, as Wild Earth always meant. The births
offered are the families within reach of the place chosen (differing by what their country
gives: a shore family, a family of the river woods, one following the herds); where no family
could live without what Wild Earth does not know, the globe says so and suggests the nearest
place where one does.

## Growing up

The player starts as a newborn in the household, in the world. Childhood runs at a **childhood
pace** (a world setting, by default about two hours of play from birth to coming of age): the
world's clock is fast between **moments**, and at normal speed in them.

- **Time passing.** Between moments the world goes on at the household tier (V2.1 §17) — the
  family's days, its moves, the seasons, brothers and sisters born, grandparents ageing — shown
  as the seasons turning over the camp while the child grows; the player watches or skips. What
  the child picks up in that time is reckoned as the household tier reckons anyone's learning:
  insight from what they were about while it was done (§11.2), the words of the language, the
  places and the people.
- **Moments.** A few minutes of play at a time, at the ages and in the doings a childhood in that
  family and culture holds (data: a curriculum of moments per life stage, culture and household
  role): carried while the mother gathers, first steps, following an older sister to the water,
  playing at knapping with a stick, watching the father strike flakes and being shown how,
  minding a little brother, gathering with the others, being taught a plant's name and use, a
  first hunt as a carrier, a rite of passage where the culture has one. The child's body is a
  child's (H3: size, reach, strength, speed by age); what the player does and learns in a moment
  is theirs (knowledge, skills, relationships with the family and the band).
- **Safe.** A child is never a target (V2.1 §1.2): nothing attacks the player as a child, and a
  child who wanders off is fetched back by whoever is minding them. Deaths in the family happen
  off-screen and are told with respect (a family in mourning, a small grave).
- **Coming of age** at the culture's age of adulthood (data; about 15 or 16 for foragers): normal
  play begins. The family goes on living — the player's parents, brothers and sisters are people
  of the world with their own lives, and the player's first relationships.

The player can skip ahead at any time (to the next moment, or to coming of age): the skipped
childhood is reckoned at the household tier.

## Later: Legacy and stepping in

When the player dies with grown children, they may go on as one of them (V2.1 §16, v2 §9.8). The
Observer's **step in** (§15.4) is a birth too: into a household the player picks on the map.
