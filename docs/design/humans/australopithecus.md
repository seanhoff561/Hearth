# *Australopithecus*

*V2.1's first species profile (v2 §8; V2-11, then H0). Data: `humans/species/species.ron`, its
population in `fauna/hominins.ron`. Code: `crates/hearth_people` (the persons and their days),
`crates/hearth/src/people.rs` (the world as they meet it), the population in `hearth_fauna`.*

## Purpose
Sporadic small bands of *Australopithecus* in suitable habitat — rare enough that meeting them is
memorable — the only other people of the timeless Wild Earth (v2 §8.1's period liberty), and the
framework's first test: the same persons, body, mind, knowledge and processes that every later
species of person runs on.

## The species
Lucy's kind: small and strongly dimorphic (a grown female about 1.1 m and 29 kg, a male 1.4 m
and 43 kg), long-armed and short-legged, a climber that walks upright, under a coat of hair. No
language — calls and gestures; plans a step deep; teaches hardly at all; no fire. Weaned at about
four, a mother at eleven or twelve, a birth every four and a half years, few living past fifty.
Its bands know the oldest stone tools' ways: a stone as a hammer, the anvil, cracking nuts,
striking sharp flakes and cutting with them, the digging stick, probing for termites, throwing
stones.

## The population
Away from the player its bands are numbers of the ecological cells (D161): an animal entry saying
what it eats, how it lives and dies, how far it ranges and who hunts it, fed, bred and killed as
the animals' groups are. It lives in the savanna–woodland mosaic and along the tropical waters of
Africa, tropical Asia, South America and Australia, some 0.15 to the km² of good land in bands of
five to twenty-five; the leopard takes it most, the lions, hyenas, crocodiles, the crowned eagle
(its young), the tiger and the jaguar now and then. The world setting **Hominin range** keeps it
to its cradle, Africa, or lets it live in all suitable habitat (the default).

## Its days
Near the player a band is drawn out as persons — the same persons each time (D167). By day they
feed under the fruit trees in season and over the open ground, drink at the water, carry marula
stones to their anvil and crack them, strike flakes now and then; at dusk they climb to the limbs
where a crown begins and bend leaf nests to sleep in, and come down at dawn. At a hunter the grown
ones call the alarm; enough of them together face it, shouting and brandishing, otherwise all go
up the trees. A person is watched from afar and fled when near or running.

## Traces, the player, and learning by watching
- The first time a band is drawn out, its site lies under the nearest nut tree: an anvil with its
  hammers and cobbles of the place's rock, the flakes and a spent core of earlier work. Their
  nests stay in the crowns.
- A calm player within sight comes to be let be over a play day and more of company; running at
  them or hunting near them undoes it. Crouched or crawling, the player is noticed less far off.
- Watching them work within 40 m teaches what the work rests on (the `watch:hominin_*` routes of
  the process's knowledge and its prerequisites), heard an hour apart at most; standing at a
  scatter of flakes and cores is studying it (`study:tool_scatter`).

- A band away lives on as the cells' numbers, reconciled with its records by their total (H7's
