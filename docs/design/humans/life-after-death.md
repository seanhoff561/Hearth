# Life after death

*V2.1 Addendum B §2 (user direction, 2026-10-03); D167. Milestones: H3 (the death, the life
story, spectating, restarting, inhabiting an adult), H8 (being born again, inhabiting a child,
eligibility and scope), H9 (the full inhabiting flow, knowledge after death, the Permadeath
preset, the old death rules retired), R3 (the same in multiplayer). Supersedes v2 §9.8 and V2.1
§16's "Legacy".*

**Built in H3 (D182):** the death as an event of the world (the player's person dies with them and
is mourned; its body lies where it fell once another is lived), the death screen telling the life,
inhabiting a grown person of the player's kin, band or the people near where they died (told by
who they are, never their looks) with the "Who you are" briefing, watching the world with a free
camera, and beginning the world again with the old save archived. Eligibility rules beyond "grown,
living, near, not another player's", the scope and knowledge-after-death settings, following a
person or animal while watching, being born again and inhabiting a child come with H8 and H9.

**Built in H8 (D200):** living on as anyone of the world lived in full or as a household, children
too (their childhood taken up at their age, safe throughout), chosen by the filters *your family*,
*your group*, *those near*, *anyone* and told by who they are; never another player's character,
nor anyone fighting, fleeing, badly hurt and dying or just giving birth; the world's **scope**
setting (anyone, kin, group and region, kin only, no one; permadeath allows no one); and **being
born again** as a baby under Legacy's rules, about where one died or a place picked on the globe —
in an era's world into one of the households offered there, in Wild Earth into one of its
families. Consent prompts for another player's family wait for multiplayer (R3); knowledge after
death, following while watching and the old rules' retirement for H9.

**In short:** when your character dies, the death is real — the body stays where it fell and its
people mourn it as their culture does — and then you choose: live on as another living person,
be born again, restart the world, or watch.

## The moment of death

- The death is an event of the world, like anyone's. The body stays where it fell. Its people
  react as their culture has it (V2.1 §7.4): mourning, a burial or other rites, the possessions
  and roles inherited by whoever the culture says.
- A **death screen** tells the life that was lived, from the person's own record (its life
  history, its kin, its relationships, its journal): its age, where it was born and to whom, its
  family and children, the people who mourn it, the places it reached, what it learned, what it
  taught.
- Then the **choice screen**. It can be opened again at any time while spectating.

## The four choices

| | Single-player | Multiplayer |
|---|---|---|
| **Inhabit another person** | Any eligible living human, adult or child (below); a child's remaining childhood follows Addendum A's flow from its age, safe throughout | The same, but a child grows up at the world's pace, with the real time it takes shown before confirming |
| **Be born again** | Addendum A's birth: a place on the globe, two to four births from real households, daughter / son / chance | **Remembered Childhood** ([multiplayer-births.md](multiplayer-births.md)) |
| **Restart the world** | **Replay this world from the beginning** (same seed and settings, a fresh history) or **Start a new world** (Create World with these settings filled in); the old save is archived, deleted only if asked | The owner and admins only; others may leave the server |
| **Spectate** | **Observer mode** (V2.1 §15.4) | A free camera following any person or animal; no time controls (admins excepted); spectators unseen, unheard and unperceived, with their own chat; speaking to the living a server setting, off by default |

### Inhabiting a person

- **Choosing.** A list filtered by *my family*, *my group*, *near where I died*, *anywhere*. Each
  entry tells only what the dead character could plausibly have known of them — their name if it
  was known, the relationship, a rough age, their group — and a stranger is told by a line about
  their household. **What they look like is never shown before choosing**, as with births.
- **Taking over.** The player takes the person's body and life whole: knowledge and skills,
  relationships, obligations, possessions, reputation, health and injuries. The person's own mind
  steps aside; to the world nothing has changed — though those who know them may notice they are
  "not themselves", through the ordinary social systems (H4).
- **"Who you are".** A briefing opens: name, age, family tree, the people who matter to them and
  how they feel about each, obligations and debts, recent memories (their episodic memory, H2),
  and their journal rebuilt from what they know.
- One inhabited nearby may find themselves at their previous character's funeral. That is meant.

*How:* a person record (D165) carries who controls it — its own mind, or a player. Inhabiting
moves the player's control to another living record; the record's mind is suspended while the
player's intents drive its body, and the server's state for that player is the record's own
components. A child's record keeps the child-safety rule in full (ground rule 2).

### Who can be inhabited

- **Never** another player's present character.
- **Only with that player's consent** (a prompt to accept or decline): anyone of another
  player's immediate family — partner, children, parents, brothers and sisters.
- **Not right now:** anyone fighting, being chased, badly hurt and dying, or giving birth — no
  last-moment escapes, and those moments kept respectful.
- **Scope**, a world (and server) setting:

| Setting | Who | Default |
|---|---|---|
| *Anyone* | any eligible living human on the planet | single-player |
| *Kin, group and region* | the dead character's family, its group, or anyone in the region where it died | multiplayer |
| *Kin only* | living relatives | — |
| *None* | no inhabiting; being born again, restarting and spectating remain | — |

### What is kept: "Knowledge after death"

| Setting | Effect | Default for |
|---|---|---|
| **Their knowledge only** | You know exactly what the new person knows; your old journal stays readable as *notes from a past life* and unlocks nothing | Authentic |
| **Head start** | What your earlier characters discovered comes back much faster: those nodes become *legends* (the knowledge state's quick-returning insight, v2 §12) | — |
| **Keep everything** | What you discovered carries over, known at a beginner's skill | Hardy |

Skills are always the new person's own; so are relationships, reputation and possessions. The
old character's are inherited as its culture says. What every past life discovered is kept per
player (not per person), so it survives any number of deaths.

### Permadeath

A preset, no longer a mode: inhabiting's scope *None* and being born again disabled. On death
one may spectate, restart, or end the world with the life story kept.

## The old death rules

v2 §9.8's three rules become presets of these settings when H9 retires them (with a save
migration): **Legacy** → inhabit *Anyone*, knowledge *Head start* (v2's "legend" of what was
learned); **Hardy** → *Keep everything*; **Permadeath** → the preset above.

## Tests (Addendum B §6)

- Inhabiting moves exactly the target's knowledge, skills, relationships, obligations and
  possessions to the player; the old character's possessions follow its culture's inheritance.
- Players' characters, unconsented kin and those in a "not right now" state are never offered.
- Restarting never deletes a save without an explicit confirmation.
- Spectators are perceived by no agent or animal, and cannot talk to the living when the setting
  is off.
- A child inhabited or born can be harmed by no source, in any setting.
